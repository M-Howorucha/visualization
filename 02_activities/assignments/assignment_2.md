    The "bad" visualization is found here: https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/kelly/viz/Badcharts2/MMs
	I classified this as "bad" for several reasons, starting with a poor aesthetic. The visualization tries to incorporate a lot of information into a small window, which makes the visualization look crowded and unclear. Additionally, the use of colour is both unclear, and confusing. There is no legend present in the visualization to define what the colours mean; the only way to tell what the colour means is by hovering over a section in the top right of the window. Additionally, many of my other concerns are perceptual, such as the use of colours as in this case it is unnecessary since it is a binary tool; comparing the USA, to every other nation. Many of the other plots/axes do not have labels to define what they are. Most notably, the primary x- and y-axes of the window do not have labels defining what the variables are. the smaller horizontal line-plots beneath the text boxes at the top also do not have labels. The vertical bar in these plots are also unlabeled so it is unclear if that is representing a mean, median, 0, etc. Lastly, in terms of honestly representing data (substantive) there is an issue with the inset plot, which defines the vertical axis as a "log" plot, but it does not accurate display the axes ticks, so the first two labels are the same, and it is not clear how different they actually are. 
	The "good" visualization can be found here: https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/riaa1295/viz/U_S_RecordedMusicSalesVolumesbyFormat_0/SalesVolumebyFormat
	I classified this as a good visualization because it is clear, concise and I believe it accurately represents the data. The use of colours is very clear in how it represents the different sources of media with the legend on the right, indicating which source is which. Additionally, the "types" of formats are clustered into similar colours, which helps in differentiating the sources. The inclusion of the source of the data increases the perceived factual basis in the visualization. Overall I would argue that there is a very low cognitive load to interpreting the visualization.

Lastly, having the ability to switch between nations, is useful, but as identified in the lecture slides, working memory is limited, so perhaps having a visualization which clearly identifies the different nations simultaneously, such that they can be compared side by side, would be useful.
